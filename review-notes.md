## Standardness issue and its independent repair

The literal statement that any tensor word containing a Y=XZ factor has trivial fixed lattice is too broad: Y1Y2 has a 64-dimensional real fixed space. Inspection of the KRR source ancillary `BWOrbifolds.nb` found that the final loops use `Tuples[{0,1,2},6]` for the remaining factors and therefore do not cover Y factors there. Those observations do not disprove the intended standardness conclusion.

Section 3 proves that conclusion independently. It derives the two nonzero generator phase quadratics from the half-Gram matrix, uses their invariance under the binary generator actions, and applies a six-variable exterior-algebra identity to show vanishing on the **entire binary fixed space** of every involution. Integral fixed vectors reduce to that space, so no unproved saturation assertion about a computed nullspace is needed. The source phase identifications are also checked coefficient by coefficient by both lift implementations. The optional exhaustive checker confirms the finite-dimensional lemma independently of the analytic derivation.

The quaternion plane condition is similarly explicit: both vectors have quadratic value one **and** their polar pairing is one. Nonsingularity of the two vectors alone would not suffice.

## Unitary extension and its precise scope

Section 7 proves unitarity of the same full-group extension, with the anomaly trivialization fixed. First, all irreducible modules of the fixed algebra are unitarizable by Lam's twisted lattice construction and Gui's Proposition 10.9. Adjoining the central magnetic simple current gives a unitary intermediate extension by Gui's Theorem 11.5. The selected noncentral modules restrict irreducibly to the original fixed algebra. An explicit lemma transports their Hermitian forms through the intermediate extension: multiplicity one reduces the adjoint discrepancy to a sign, and the leading vacuum term in the mode commutator excludes the negative sign. A second use of Gui's Theorem 11.5 makes the full extension unitary.

The central decomposition is checked using the actual multiplication of the fixed algebra C[E]. There are two possible fixed-point resolutions for each relevant induced module; the multiplication chooses one consistently, and the proof does not claim uniqueness of those choices. The 16,384 selected currents form E/<z>, including the intermediate vacuum. Neither complete unitarity of the nonabelian fixed algebra nor a VOA–conformal-net comparison is assumed. The commutator lemma is proved in full, not attributed to Lam or Gui; no literature-priority claim is made for it.

## Exact weight-four minimum and uniform family

Section 8 determines the minimum over the same globally coherent family of inflated discrete-torsion cocycles in one orbifold-compatible category. Every member is unitary by the central simple-current argument and contains the common fixed algebra $W=V_{BW(7)}^E$. The proposed conclusion is
$$
\min_B\dim(T_B)_4=\dim W_4=4\,925\,572,
$$
attained by a member with exactly $4\,925\,570$ weight-four Virasoro primaries. The two remaining weight-four states are independent vacuum descendants. This is a minimum within the stated full-orbifold family, not among all holomorphic VOAs of central charge 128.

The argument first constructs an actual Cartesian $J_{\mathrm{lin}}=GL(7,2)$ lift using Steinberg's presentation and a common-support calculation on at most four tensor positions. It derives scalarity of each entire noncentral-involution ground space from the physical defect operators and actual normalizer transport. The relative braided-equivalence torsor retains the specified tensor copy of $\operatorname{Rep}(E)$; its genuine affine $J_{\mathrm{lin}}$-action and the finite-group cohomology inputs give a unique fixed isomorphism class. Moving to that class translates the global torsion parameter and leaves the family unchanged. Positive ribbon compatibility makes the resulting ordinary ground characters eligible. Their stabilizers constrain the labels, leaving one common binary parameter on the mixed orbit, without requiring that parameter to be computed.

One global form then excludes every possible weight-four twisted contribution. It uses $C$ equal to multiplication by $t$ on $\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^7+t+1)$ and
$$
B_C((x,f),(u,h))=h(Cx)+f(Cu).
$$
Both $C$ and $C+I$ are invertible, so the exclusion works for both possible values of the common mixed-orbit parameter. The choice is explicit relative to the unique $J_{\mathrm{lin}}$-fixed isomorphism class of relative equivalences. The proof does not give numerical coordinates for that class in an older cochain gauge. It does not assert a $J_{\mathrm{lin}}$-action on the resulting extension, a full character, or a count of inequivalent theories. The same global choice is used in every sector; no independent sector normalization or optimization search is a premise. Separate AI agents reviewed the lift and scalarity, relative action and cohomology, and final exclusion steps. Those reviews do not constitute external validation or formal verification of the spectral conclusion.

The untwisted and defect counts use Griess, [*Pieces of $2^d$*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2004.08.014), and Bakalov–Kac, [*Twisted Modules over Lattice Vertex Algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1142/9789812702562_0001). Griess's Notation 8.7 and Proposition 8.9 are cited in the author manuscript dated 11 August 2004; the numbering differs between versions. The later [correction, arXiv:math/0511277v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511277v1), was also read: it revises uniqueness and generation arguments and an appendix lemma, without replacing the minimal-vector count or orbit statement used here. Bakalov–Kac's even-unimodular twisted-module uniqueness is also used for physical normalizer transport. All consulted versions are pinned in `sources.json`.

Section 9 extends the action-specific anomaly proof to every odd parameter $m\ge5$, with an actual order-four cyclic obstruction at $m=3$. Its unitary full orbifolds have gap four for odd $m\ge7$ and exactly $(2^m+1)(2^m+2)/6$ weight-four primaries for odd $m\ge9$. The boundary checks matter: the block lemma requires even half-Gram restrictions, already valid at $m=5$; the exterior standardness proof needs only $m-1>2$; and the cited cohomology filtration applies for $m\ge3$. Two separate agents reviewed those thresholds and the resulting spectrum. No finite enumeration at higher ranks is a premise.

Higher-charge existence alone is already available by iterating a swap orbifold of a suitable gap-four seed. The comparison in Section 9.4 uses [Barron–Dong–Mason, arXiv:math/9803118v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9803118v2), proof of Theorem 3.9, and [van Ekeren–Möller–Scheithauer, arXiv:1507.08142v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.08142v3), Theorem 5.16 and its type-zero specialization. Both are published sources, and the cyclic extension theorem applies at general central charge. Fix the minimizing full-$E(7)$ seed $T_0$ above, with $P_4(T_0)=4\,925\,570$. Each swap increases that number by one, so at matching central charge $2^m$ the exact comparison is
$$
P_4(S^{m-7}(T_0))=4\,925\,570+(m-7),\qquad
P_4(T_m)=\frac{(2^m+1)(2^m+2)}6.
$$
The counts differ for every odd $m\ge9$, proving nonisomorphism for this fixed seed and the stated direct family. The seed's exact count is specific to the selected full-$E(7)$ orbifold retaining the given fixed algebra, not an assertion about arbitrary central-charge-128 seeds. The comparison is limited to these constructions and is not an exhaustive novelty result.
