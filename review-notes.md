The proposed argument, code and exposition in this packet were generated by coordinated AI agents. One agent developed the compatible quaternion-lift route and Cartesian calculation. A separate agent reconstructed the matrices directly from the source formulas, checked all quadratic lift phases, and supplied the analytic involution-standardness argument. A subsequent block-cocycle argument replaced the finite lift calculation as the logical justification of the specific quaternion compatibility step; two other agents checked its character argument, Gram divisibility and integral row formula independently. The subsequent unitarity argument received separate audits of its formal contragredient construction, leading commutator sign, and actual central-extension module decomposition. This separation is useful internal evidence; it is not external expert or human validation.

Keller, Ashley Winter Roberts and Jeremy Roberts supplied the Barnes–Wall action, the conditional construction, the twisted-module weights and the low-weight spectral analysis. Johnson–Freyd and Treumann supplied the extraspecial-group cohomology filtration used here. In particular, their proof concerning the sporadic group J4 already uses a quaternion subgroup inside a binary dihedral group to eliminate a cohomology class (printed page 30). The general idea of quaternion restriction is therefore prior work. The potentially new step here is its realization by compatible lattice-VOA lifts for the **specified** KRR action; the draft makes no general-method priority claim.

## Standardness issue and its independent repair

The literal statement that any tensor word containing a Y=XZ factor has trivial fixed lattice is too broad: Y1Y2 has a 64-dimensional real fixed space. Inspection of the KRR source ancillary `BWOrbifolds.nb` found that the final loops use `Tuples[{0,1,2},6]` for the remaining factors and therefore do not cover Y factors there. Those observations do not disprove the intended standardness conclusion.

Section 3 proves that conclusion independently. It derives the two nonzero generator phase quadratics from the half-Gram matrix, uses their invariance under the binary generator actions, and applies a six-variable exterior-algebra identity to show vanishing on the **entire binary fixed space** of every involution. Integral fixed vectors reduce to that space, so no unproved saturation assertion about a computed nullspace is needed. The source phase identifications are also checked coefficient by coefficient by both lift implementations. The optional exhaustive checker confirms the finite-dimensional lemma independently of the analytic derivation.

The quaternion plane condition is similarly explicit: both vectors have quadratic value one **and** their polar pairing is one. Nonsingularity of the two vectors alone would not suffice.

## What has and has not been checked

Section 4 supplies an analytic proof of the lift compatibility. The half-Gram form vanishes modulo two within each of sixteen eight-dimensional coordinate blocks. All required isometries preserve those blocks, and two genuine lifts of the same isometry differ by a lattice character. These facts force the canonical lifts to form a homomorphism on the relevant subgroup. A two-term row formula proves integrality of the order-eight generator directly. The finite code is therefore corroboration, not a logical dependency of that step.

The primary checker uses arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic, verifies the cocycle-lift identity, and checks every coefficient of the quadratic phase differences for the four stated relations. This is a complete finite check of those identities; it is not a sample of lattice vectors. The alternative code starts in Cartesian coordinates, converts through the lattice basis and reaches matching matrix hashes without importing the primary implementation. Its NumPy signed-integer arithmetic is secondary evidence; the arbitrary-precision checker is the minimal finite certificate.

The cohomology, standardness, block-lift, anomaly naturality, VOA extension, unitarity and spectrum steps remain mathematical arguments requiring reading and assessment. They have not been encoded in a formal proof kernel. The finite reports concern width seven; they do not establish the uniform theorem, unitarity or uniqueness. Section 9 establishes the parameter dependence analytically. The exact-gap witness in Section 6 is an explicit invariant harmonic quartic, so its primary property does not implicitly assume a unitary extension.

The finite code contains no generic inhomogeneous linear-system solver. The exploratory solver and large search outputs were omitted rather than added to the proof's trusted components.

## Unitary extension and its precise scope

Section 7 proves unitarity of the same full-group extension, with the anomaly trivialization fixed. First, all irreducible modules of the fixed algebra are unitarizable by Lam's twisted lattice construction and Gui's Proposition 10.9. Adjoining the central magnetic simple current gives a unitary intermediate extension by Gui's Theorem 11.5. The selected noncentral modules restrict irreducibly to the original fixed algebra. An explicit lemma transports their Hermitian forms through the intermediate extension: multiplicity one reduces the adjoint discrepancy to a sign, and the leading vacuum term in the mode commutator excludes the negative sign. A second use of Gui's Theorem 11.5 makes the full extension unitary.

The central decomposition is checked using the actual multiplication of the fixed algebra C[E]. There are two possible fixed-point resolutions for each relevant induced module; the multiplication chooses one consistently, and the proof does not claim uniqueness of those choices. The 16,384 selected currents form E/<z>, including the intermediate vacuum. Neither complete unitarity of the nonabelian fixed algebra nor a VOA–conformal-net comparison is assumed. The commutator lemma is proved in full, not attributed to Lam or Gui; no literature-priority claim is made for it.

## Multiplicity bound and uniform family

Section 8 bounds the lightest-state multiplicity over a globally coherent family of inflated discrete-torsion cocycles in one fixed orbifold-compatible category. Every member is unitary by the central simple-current argument and contains the same fixed algebra. Consequently
$$
4\,925\,572\le\min_B\dim(T_B)_4\le5\,057\,652,
$$
with at most $5\,057\,650$ weight-four primaries for some member. The upper bound combines the minimal-vector count and orbit structure, an explicit 64-pair defect calculation, and uniform averaging over 4096 distinct centralizer characters for each relevant class. It assumes neither a trivial ground-space centralizer representation nor independence between classes. It does not identify an optimizing cocycle, determine a full character, or count inequivalent theories. Separate agents audited the actual algebra and unitarity, the character-multiplicity average, and the lattice and defect inputs.

Those counting inputs use Griess, [*Pieces of $2^d$*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2004.08.014), and Bakalov–Kac, [*Twisted Modules over Lattice Vertex Algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1142/9789812702562_0001). Griess's Notation 8.7 and Proposition 8.9 are cited in the author manuscript dated 11 August 2004; the numbering differs between versions. The later [correction, arXiv:math/0511277v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511277v1), was also read: it revises uniqueness and generation arguments and an appendix lemma, without replacing the minimal-vector count or orbit statement used here. All consulted versions are pinned in `sources.json`.

Section 9 extends the action-specific anomaly proof to every odd parameter $m\ge5$, with an actual order-four cyclic obstruction at $m=3$. Its unitary full orbifolds have gap four for odd $m\ge7$ and exactly $(2^m+1)(2^m+2)/6$ weight-four primaries for odd $m\ge9$. The boundary checks matter: the block lemma requires even half-Gram restrictions, already valid at $m=5$; the exterior standardness proof needs only $m-1>2$; and the cited cohomology filtration applies for $m\ge3$. Two separate agents reviewed those thresholds and the resulting spectrum. No finite enumeration at higher ranks is a premise.

Higher-charge existence alone is already available by iterating a swap orbifold of a suitable gap-four seed. The comparison in Section 9.4 uses [Barron–Dong–Mason, arXiv:math/9803118v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9803118v2), proof of Theorem 3.9, and [van Ekeren–Möller–Scheithauer, arXiv:1507.08142v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.08142v3), Theorem 5.16 and its type-zero specialization. Both are published sources, and the cyclic extension theorem applies at general central charge. One fixed sparse full-$E(7)$ seed has between $4\,925\,570$ and $5\,057\,650$ weight-four primaries; each swap increases that number by one. This proves nonisomorphism with the direct family for every odd $m\ge9$. The lower bound is specific to full-$E(7)$ seeds retaining the stated fixed algebra, and the comparison is limited to this sequence of constructions. It is not an exhaustive novelty result.

2. Ji and Tian, [*CFTs with Large Virasoro Primary Gaps*, arXiv:2611.23231v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23231v1), 17 November 2026, prove a related full, nonchiral construction. Their Theorem 6.1 inflates the chiral anomaly and cancels it on the left/right fiber-product symmetry. That does not establish that the individual chiral anomaly vanishes. This preprint is context only, not a dependency of the present proof.
3. Höhn, [*An Extremal Vertex Operator Algebra of Central Charge c=64*, Hexagon 2611.00631v2](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00631v2), 20 November 2026, claims a different central-charge-64 construction. Its §1.2 explicitly describes the KRR central-charge-128 construction as conditional on anomaly vanishing. We have not independently validated Höhn's full construction and use it only as contemporary status and overlap evidence.

These related results make “first CFT with gap above two” and similarly broad priority language inappropriate. The proposed result is the specified chiral anomaly statement and its stated orbifold consequence.
