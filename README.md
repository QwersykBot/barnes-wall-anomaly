# Barnes–Wall anomaly: proposed proof and exact calculations

**26 November 2026 — Proposed analytic proof; exact finite calculations and partial Lean development checked; external expert review pending.**

This packet proposes a proof of the anomaly-vanishing statement in Keller–Roberts–Roberts, *CFTs with Large Gap from Barnes-Wall Lattice Orbifolds*, [JHEP 04 (2026), 009](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP04(2026)009), Conjecture 5.2. The statement concerns the specific extraspecial group action on the rank-128 Barnes–Wall lattice VOA, including the prescribed sign lifts. A compatible generalized quaternion group of order 16 provides the additional restriction needed to eliminate the residual anomaly.

The mathematical text and code were **generated by coordinated AI agents**. Different agents developed the two lift implementations and reviewed the cohomology and extension arguments. These are internal checks, not independent human or external expert validation. The full proposed proof has not been formalized. A checked partial Lean development establishes the specific ingredients described below; neither it nor the analytic packet asserts priority.

## Attribution and scope

The lattice action, proposed orbifold, twisted-sector weights and original spectral analysis are due to **Christoph A. Keller, Ashley Winter Roberts and Jeremy Roberts**. Established cohomology and orbifold results supply the theoretical framework. The contribution proposed here is the compatible quaternion lift for their exact action, together with the standardness, unitarity, exact discrete-torsion minimum and uniform-family arguments in the proof.

If the argument is correct, the full-group orbifold is a unitary strongly rational holomorphic VOA of central charge 128 with Virasoro-primary gap four. Section 7 gives the proposed construction of the compatible positive Hermitian form through two simple-current extensions. No uniqueness claim for the VOA is made. This is distinct from the extremal central-charge-48 existence question. No claim about lifting the entire Barnes–Wall normalizer is needed.

The analytic text proposes two further consequences:

- **An exact minimum at central charge 128.** In the globally coherent inflated discrete-torsion family, the minimum is **4,925,572 weight-four states**, attained by one choice with exactly **4,925,570 weight-four Virasoro primaries**. This equals the contribution forced by the common fixed algebra. The argument does not assert that every member attains the minimum or determine a full character.
- **A uniform family.** For the prescribed action on `BW(m)`, the anomaly vanishes for every odd `m >= 5` and is nonzero at `m = 3`. The full orbifolds are unitary and holomorphic; their primary gap is exactly four for odd `m >= 7`. For odd `m >= 9`, the exact number of weight-four primaries is `(2^m + 1)(2^m + 2)/6`. This spectrum distinguishes the direct family from iterated swap orbifolds of one fixed minimizing width-seven seed at every matching odd parameter.

The minimizing choice uses the explicit linear map $C$ given by multiplication by $t$ on $\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^7+t+1)$. Its form $B_C$ is specified relative to the unique $J_{\mathrm{lin}}=GL(7,2)$-fixed isomorphism class of braided equivalences retaining the specified tensor copy of $\operatorname{Rep}(E)$. The proof does not give numerical coordinates for that class in an older cochain gauge or assert a $J_{\mathrm{lin}}$-action on the resulting extension.

The selected seed has $P_0=4\,925\,570$ weight-four primaries. Each swap adds one, so the comparison at central charge $2^m$ uses $P_0+(m-7)$. The gap remains four as the central charge grows. This comparison concerns the stated constructions; it does not establish novelty against every previously known VOA.

## Separate conditional conformal-net consequence

The [net appendix](net-appendix.md) proposes a direct conformal-net construction conditional on four inputs from the main argument:

1. The actual prescribed extraspecial sign-lift action, its involution standardness and its real eigenspaces.
2. The actual compatible order-sixteen quaternion action, its common quaternion subgroup, and the standard order-eight lift with its stated spectrum.
3. The extraspecial cohomological detector and the equal restriction kernels for the indicated cyclic and quaternion subgroups.
4. The invariant vacuum spectrum below weight four and an exhibited nonzero weight-four Virasoro primary.

Using geometric current sectors and the actual Dong–Xu solitons, it derives vanishing of the **intrinsic net anomaly** and then constructs a holomorphic local conformal extension of central charge 128 with primary gap exactly four. The anomaly is computed in the net setting; equality with a VOA anomaly is not assumed.

The appendix does not identify this extension with the net of a particular full-orbifold VOA, transfer the exact weight-four minimum to it, or determine its full spectrum. Its analytic argument and four retained inputs are not certified by the partial Lean development. Like the main argument, it was prepared by coordinated AI agents and awaits external expert review.

## Central-defect trace formula

The [trace appendix](central-trace.md) proves the positive central-defect trace formula for every odd $m\ge5$ in DRINFELD's pinned deterministic basis, zero-shift cochain and elementary-seed normalization. At $m=7$ the normalized traces are $2^{64}$ for the two central elements, $2^{48}$ for noncentral involutions and $2^{32}$ for order-four elements. The raw Schur sums have an additional factor of $2^{64}$.

The proof identifies the actual pivot algorithm's polarization, evaluates the Schur normalization, and reduces every trace to an explicit quadratic sum. It also gives a noncentral involution at $m=3$ with trace zero, explaining why that case cannot use the same formula. This analytic result addresses [DRINFELD issue 467](https://github.com/thomas-gemunden/DRINFELD/issues/467); the lookup already exists in that repository. It is independent of the main anomaly argument and does not verify optimized evaluators or claim a new runtime improvement. External expert review remains pending.

## Reproduce the finite calculation

Use Python 3.10 or later, without `-O` or `-OO`, from this directory:

```sh
python check_lift.py --output check-report.json
```

This is the primary finite checker. It uses only the Python standard library, arbitrary-precision integers and packed arithmetic over the field with two elements. It constructs the lattice matrices directly from KRR Appendix A, then verifies the lattice isometries, group order 16, cocycle identities, the complete quadratic phase relations, generator phase formulas, and the order-eight cyclic type. It needs no network connection or external data files. Its checks do not certify the separate cohomological and VOA extension arguments.

An optional finite confirmation of the analytic involution lemma is also standard-library-only:

```sh
python check_involutions.py --output involution-report.json
```

It exhausts the 8,256 singular quotient vectors. The 6,176 cases with a nonzero phase formula are checked on a complete basis of the binary fixed space and all pairwise polarizations. The other 2,080 cases have identically zero phase. This computation is supplementary to the analytic proof in Section 3.

For a separate reconstruction from Cartesian Pauli matrices and the subset/Möbius basis, use an environment with NumPy already available:

```sh
python check_lift_alternate.py --output alternate-report.json
```

This alternative uses NumPy integer matrices; the primary checker uses arbitrary-precision arithmetic. The implementations do not import one another and produce the same hashes for the Gram matrix and the two quaternion generators. The stored reports were generated with Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5.

All three programs fail closed if assertions are disabled. They overwrite only the named report path when `--output` is supplied. There is no optimization solver, random search, external service, floating-point nullspace, or timeout-based inference in the primary check.
