AN ELEMENTARY SIMPLICITY CRITERION FOR AN EXTRASPECIAL INVOLUTION CENTRALIZER
26 November 2026

Scope
-----
This is a finite-group lemma. In its intended VOA application, finiteness of
the automorphism group, the exact full involution centralizer, its module
action, and the stated fusion must be established independently. The lemma
does not establish those inputs and does not identify the resulting simple
group as E8(2).

The argument uses the ordinary Sylow theorems, with the normalizer condition
and Frattini argument recalled below, plus elementary group and linear
algebra. It requires no classification or recognition theorem.

THEOREM
-------
Let H be a finite group and z an involution. Put C = C_H(z). Suppose there is
a normal subgroup Q of C with the following properties:

(1) Q is extraspecial of order 2^(1+2n), where n >= 2, and
    Z(Q) = Q' = <z>.

(2) C_C(Q) = <z>.

(3) C/Q is a nonabelian simple group.

(4) The conjugation action of C on the F2-vector space Q/<z> is irreducible.

(5) There is an involution t in Q\<z> such that z, t, and zt are all
    conjugate in H.

Then H is nonabelian simple.

In (5), z and t commute because z is central in Q, so they generate a
four-group. An element of order three cycling its three nonidentity elements
is sufficient, but the proof needs only their conjugacy. The plus/minus type
of Q is immaterial to this lemma.

ELEMENTARY FACTS USED
--------------------
A. Normalizer condition. If U is a proper subgroup of a finite p-group P,
then U < N_P(U). Indeed, let U act by left multiplication on the cosets P/U.
The number of fixed cosets is |N_P(U):U|; all other orbit lengths are
divisible by p. Since |P:U| is divisible by p and there is at least one fixed
coset, there are at least p fixed cosets.

B. Frattini argument. If N is normal in a finite H and S is a Sylow subgroup
of N, then H = N N_H(S). Conjugation by any element of H sends S to another
Sylow subgroup of N, and Sylow conjugacy inside N supplies the adjustment
which normalizes S. Normality also gives H = N_H(S) N.

C. Fixed-point-free involutions. If alpha is an automorphism of a finite
group A, alpha^2 = 1, and C_A(alpha) = 1, then alpha acts by inversion.
To prove this, the map f(x) = x^(-1) alpha(x) is injective: equality f(x)=f(y)
makes y x^(-1) fixed by alpha, hence x=y. It is therefore surjective, and
alpha(f(x)) = f(x)^(-1). Thus alpha(a)=a^(-1) for every a in A. Inversion
being an automorphism also forces A to be abelian.

Consequently a four-group cannot act on a nontrivial finite group with all
three nonidentity elements acting fixed-point-freely: two of the actions
would both be inversion, and their product would be the identity action.

D. Extraspecial centralizers. If Q is as in (1) and x is noncentral in Q,
then

    [C_Q(x), C_Q(x)] = Z(Q).

The commutator induces a nondegenerate alternating form on V=Q/Z(Q).
The image of C_Q(x) is the perpendicular hyperplane of the nonzero vector
xZ(Q). This hyperplane has radical <xZ(Q)> and restricted symplectic rank
2n-2. Since n>=2, its restricted form is nonzero. Commutators in C_Q(x)
therefore attain the nonidentity element of Z(Q), proving the assertion.
The condition n>=2 is essential to this step: at width one the perpendicular
hyperplane is one-dimensional and its alternating form is zero.

PROOF OF THE THEOREM
--------------------
1. Sylow subgroups of C are Sylow subgroups of H.

Take S in Syl_2(C). Since Q is a normal 2-subgroup of C, Q <= S. Therefore

    Z(S) <= C_C(Q) = <z>.

Conversely z is central in C and belongs to S, so Z(S)=<z>. If a Sylow
2-subgroup P of H containing S were larger than S, Fact A would give
N_P(S)>S. Every element of N_P(S) preserves Z(S)=<z> and hence centralizes
z. Thus N_P(S) would be a 2-subgroup of C properly containing S, contrary
to the choice of S. It follows that S is Sylow in H.

2. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of H contains Q.

Let 1 != N be normal in H and put R=N cap Q. It is normal in C. By (4),
the image R<z>/<z> is either zero or all of Q/<z>. In the latter case
R<z>=Q and, because <z> is central,

    [R,R] = [R<z>,R<z>] = [Q,Q] = <z>.

Thus <z> <= R, and then R=Q. In the former case R is either 1 or <z>.
Hence the only possibilities are R=1, <z>, or Q.

The possibility R=<z> is excluded by (5): z in N and normality of N imply
t in N, while t in Q\<z> contradicts R=<z>.

Suppose R=1. The subgroup K=N cap C is normal in C, so [K,Q] <= K cap Q=1.
By (2), K <= <z>, and its trivial intersection with Q forces K=1.
Equivalently, C_N(z)=1. By normality of N and the conjugacy in (5), also
C_N(t)=C_N(zt)=1. The four-group <z,t> would therefore act on the nontrivial
finite group N with all three nonidentity elements acting fixed-point-freely,
contrary to Fact C. This excludes R=1 and proves Q <= N.

3. The centralizer of z in N cannot be only Q.

By Step 2, C_N(z)=N cap C is a normal subgroup of C containing Q. Assumption
(3) leaves just two possibilities:

    C_N(z) = Q, or C_N(z) = C.

Assume C_N(z)=Q. First Q is Sylow in N. Otherwise, if Q<P for some Sylow
2-subgroup P of N, Fact A supplies N_P(Q)>Q. But an element normalizing Q
preserves its center <z> and hence lies in C_N(z)=Q, a contradiction.

Moreover N_H(Q)=C: the inclusion C<=N_H(Q) follows from Q normal in C;
the reverse inclusion follows from Z(Q)=<z>. Fact B now gives

    H = N C = C N.

Thus the H-conjugate t of z is already an N-conjugate: write a conjugating
element as c u with c in C and u in N, to obtain t=z^u. Put

    Q_t = C_N(t) = Q^u.

The commuting involutions z and t belong to both centralizers where needed,
and

    R0 = Q cap Q_t = C_Q(t) = C_(Q_t)(z).

Here t is noncentral in Q, whose center is <z>, and z is noncentral in Q_t,
whose center is <t>. Both extraspecial groups have width n>=2. Fact D
applied to these two descriptions of the same subgroup gives

    R0' = <z>  and  R0' = <t>,

contradicting t not in <z>. Therefore C_N(z)=C, and C<=N.

4. N is all of H.

Let S be as in Step 1. Since S<=C<=N and S is Sylow in H, it is also Sylow
in N. Fact B yields H=N N_H(S). Every element normalizing S preserves
Z(S)=<z>, so N_H(S)<=C<=N. Hence H=N.

Every nontrivial normal subgroup of H is therefore H itself. Since H
contains the nonabelian extraspecial group Q, H is nonabelian simple. QED.

APPLICATION TO THE PROPOSED VOA AUTOMORPHISM GROUP
------------------------------------------------
For the intended application take n=28, Q of plus type and order 2^57,
and C/Q=E7(2). The hypotheses to import from the separate VOA/group work are:

* H=Aut(Y) is finite;
* C_H(z) is the full centralizer with the specified normal Q;
* C_C(Q)=<z>;
* its quotient is E7(2), and its action on Q/<z> is irreducible;
* an actual automorphism supplies fusion of z,t,zt for a four-group
  <z,t><=Q, not merely a permutation of abstract categorical labels.

Faithfulness alone does not replace irreducibility in the normal-subgroup
argument. The categorical extraspecial identification and actual fusion
are separate inputs. Once these inputs are established, simplicity is an
elementary conclusion; recognition of the resulting simple group remains a
separate theorem application.

Source and review status
------------------------
No external theorem beyond the ordinary Sylow theorems is required by this
proof; the other finite-group facts used are proved above. In particular,
the finite-simple hypothesis of the Aschbacher ICM1978 classification theorem
can be discharged by this lemma when the listed inputs hold.
