RECOGNITION OF E8(2) FROM AN EXTRASPECIAL INVOLUTION CENTRALIZER
26 November 2026

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
This note supplies the finite-group recognition step for a proposed VOA
automorphism group. It does not prove the VOA construction, finiteness of
its automorphism group, the full-centralizer identification, or actual
fusion of involutions. Those are separate inputs. No splitting of an
extension denoted by a dot is assumed.

THEOREM. Let H be finite, let z be an involution, and put C=C_H(z).
Suppose Q is normal in C and the following hold:

 (i) Q is extraspecial of order 2^57, with Z(Q)=Q'=<z>;
 (ii) C_C(Q)=<z> and C/Q is isomorphic to E7(2);
 (iii) the action of C on V=Q/<z>, a 56-dimensional F2-space, is
       irreducible;
 (iv) some involution t in Q\<z> has z,t,zt all conjugate in H.

Then H is isomorphic to E8(2).

The intended Q has plus type. The argument below does not need to assume
its type separately. An actual order-three automorphism cycling z,t,zt
is sufficient for (iv), but a permutation of abstract module labels is
not sufficient. Hypothesis (iii) can be verified from the specific module
identification in the proposed application by the short argument in
Section 1. Faithfulness alone is not a substitute for irreducibility.

The proof has three parts: an elementary simplicity reduction, application
of the standalone classification theorem stated by Aschbacher in ICM1978,
and elementary order exclusions. The classification theorem, rather than
centralizer shape alone, is the non-elementary recognition input.

1. VERIFYING THE MODULE HYPOTHESIS IN THE INTENDED APPLICATION

The polar commutator form on V=Q/Z(Q) is nondegenerate alternating and
C-invariant. In the VOA application, the actual C/Q-action must be
identified equivariantly with the 56-dimensional fusion-label module D
in [H], or its dual. The invariant nondegenerate form identifies this
module with its dual.

[H, Theorem 7.2, printed p.20] gives the five nonzero orbit sizes:

  277347807; 37518401085984; 35991278474747904;
  18612496260661248; 17416300624084992.

Their minimum is 277347807 > 2^28-1 = 268435455. A nonzero invariant
subspace W contains an entire nonzero orbit, so dim(W)>=29. If W were
proper, its perpendicular space W_perp would be a nonzero invariant
subspace, and nondegeneracy would give

  1 <= dim(W_perp)=56-dim(W) <= 27.

This contradicts the same orbit lower bound. Thus V is irreducible. This
argument uses the actual orbit data, not just the abstract names E7(2)
and F2^56.

2. ELEMENTARY SIMPLICITY AND THE SYLOW 2-ORDER

We use the Sylow theorems and two elementary consequences. A proper
subgroup U of a finite p-group P has U<N_P(U): acting by U on P/U, the
fixed cosets number |N_P(U):U|, which is positive and divisible by p.
Also, for N normal in H and S a Sylow subgroup of N, Sylow conjugacy
inside N gives the Frattini argument H=N N_H(S)=N_H(S)N.

2.1. Let S be Sylow in C at the prime 2. Since Q is normal in C, Q<=S.
Therefore Z(S)<=C_C(Q)=<z>, while z is central in C. Hence Z(S)=<z>.
If S lay properly inside a 2-subgroup P of H, N_P(S)>S would preserve
Z(S)=<z>, and would be a 2-subgroup of C properly containing S. This
is impossible. Thus S is Sylow in H.

The ATLAS order [A-E7] has 2-part 2^63. Consequently

  |H|_2=|C|_2=|Q| |E7(2)|_2=2^(57+63)=2^120.          (2.1)

2.2. Let 1!=N be normal in H and set R=N intersect Q. Irreducibility
implies that RZ(Q)/Z(Q) is zero or all of V. In the latter case RZ(Q)=Q,
and centrality of Z(Q) gives

  [R,R]=[RZ(Q),RZ(Q)]=[Q,Q]=Z(Q),

so R=Q. In the former case R is 1 or <z>. The case R=<z> contradicts
fusion: z in N forces t in N, while t is in Q\<z>.

If R=1, put K=N intersect C. Then [K,Q]<=K intersect Q=1, so
K<=C_C(Q)=<z> and K=1. Thus C_N(z)=1, and fusion gives
C_N(t)=C_N(zt)=1 as well. This is impossible for a nontrivial finite N.
Indeed a fixed-point-free involutory automorphism alpha acts by inversion:
the map x -> x^(-1)alpha(x) is injective, hence surjective, and alpha
inverts every value of this map. Two such involutions both act as
inversion, so their product acts identically, whereas the third nonidentity
element of <z,t> was also supposed to act fixed-point-freely. Thus Q<=N.

2.3. Since E7(2) is nonabelian simple, C_N(z)/Q normal in C/Q implies
C_N(z)=Q or C. Suppose C_N(z)=Q. An element normalizing Q preserves
Z(Q)=<z>. The normalizer condition therefore makes Q a Sylow 2-subgroup
of N, and also N_H(Q)=C. The Frattini argument gives H=NC=CN. Hence t,
being H-conjugate to z, is already N-conjugate to z: t=z^u for some u in N.
Then Q_t=C_N(t)=Q^u is extraspecial with center <t>, and

  R0=Q intersect Q_t=C_Q(t)=C_(Q_t)(z).

In an extraspecial group of order 2^(1+2n), n>=2, the centralizer of a
noncentral element has derived subgroup equal to the center of the AMBIENT
extraspecial group. To see this,
its image in the symplectic quotient is a perpendicular hyperplane; that
hyperplane has restricted symplectic rank 2n-2>0, so its commutators attain
the nonidentity central element. Here n=28. Applying this fact to both
descriptions of R0 gives R0'=<z> and R0'=<t>, a contradiction. Therefore
C<=N.

2.4. Now S<=C<=N, and S is Sylow in H and N. Every element of N_H(S)
preserves Z(S)=<z>, so N_H(S)<=C<=N. The Frattini argument gives H=N.
Thus H is nonabelian simple. This argument uses no classification theorem.

3. THE PRECISE CLASSIFICATION INPUT AND ITS HYPOTHESES

Primary input [ICM] is the final displayed theorem on printed p.283 of
Michael Aschbacher, "A Survey of the Classification Program for Finite
Simple Groups of Even Characteristic",
Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians, Helsinki
1978, volume 1, pp.281-284. Its statement is paraphrased here.

A finite simple group with a 2-local subgroup M such that
C_M(O_2(M))<=O_2(M), and such that O_2(M) has no noncyclic characteristic
abelian subgroup, belongs to this list:

 * simple groups of Lie type over the field of order two;
 * G2(3), U4(3), L4(3), Omega8+(3), A9, L2(2^n +/- 1);
 * M11, M12, M24, HS, He, Sz, J2, J3, J4, Co2, Co1,
   F24, F5, F3, F2, F1.

This is the standalone final theorem on that page, not the preceding
characteristic-2-type theorem. No hypothesis of global characteristic
2-type, rank at least three, or all proper subgroups being known is imported.
The older names in the sporadic list are decoded in Section 6 below.

All hypotheses hold with M=C:

 * H is finite simple by the assumed finiteness and Section 2.
 * C=N_H(<z>), so C is a 2-local subgroup.
 * O_2(C)=Q: the image of a normal 2-subgroup in the nonabelian simple
   quotient E7(2) is trivial.
 * C_C(O_2(C))=<z><=Q by hypothesis.
 * If A is a characteristic abelian subgroup of Q, it is normal in C.
   The image AZ(Q)/Z(Q) is invariant, so irreducibility makes it zero
   or all of V. The latter would give AZ(Q)=Q, which is abelian because
   A is abelian and Z(Q) is central, a contradiction. Thus A<=Z(Q),
   which is cyclic. There is no noncyclic characteristic abelian subgroup.

The conclusion is now an exact candidate list. The rest of the proof
eliminates candidates by their orders, without invoking an unpublished
or inaccessible type-E recognition statement.

4. GROUPS OF LIE TYPE OVER F2

Write ell for Lie rank. For the classical families, the 2-adic exponents
of the simple-group orders at q=2 are as follows, apart from the familiar
very small non-simple/derived-group cases, which cannot approach 120:

  Family                         v2(order)          v2(order)=120
  A_ell(2), ^2A_ell(2)           ell(ell+1)/2        ell=15
  B_ell(2), C_ell(2)             ell^2              no solution
  D_ell(2), ^2D_ell(2)           ell(ell-1)          no solution

For A, 15*16/2=120. The integer 120 is not a square. For D,
11*10=110<120<12*11=132. Consequently the only classical possibilities
are L16(2)=PSL_16(2) and U16(2)=PSU_16(2).

Here are the underlying order formulas and quotient conventions. Counting
ordered bases gives

  |GL_n(q)|=q^(n(n-1)/2) product_(i=1..n)(q^i-1).

At q=2, GL_n(2)=SL_n(2)=PSL_n(2). For the full unitary group [FNP,
Section 2.1],

  |U(n,q)|=q^(n(n-1)/2) product_(i=1..n)(q^i-(-1)^i).

The determinant image has order q+1; passing next to PSU divides by
gcd(n,q+1). At q=2 both divisions are odd, so they do not change v2.
For symplectic and even orthogonal groups [FNP, Sections 2.2-2.3],

  |Sp_(2ell)(q)|=q^(ell^2) product_(i=1..ell)(q^(2i)-1),
  |O_(2ell)^eps(q)|=2 q^(ell(ell-1))(q^ell-eps)
                         product_(i=1..ell-1)(q^(2i)-1).

At even q, Omega has index two in O [FNP, printed p.75]. The remaining
projective-center factor is odd (and at q=2 is trivial). Odd-dimensional
orthogonal groups in characteristic two give the symplectic family.
These formulas include the minus orthogonal twist ^2D_ell. The unitary
formula includes ^2A_ell; its parameter q=2 uses matrices over F4.

All exceptional and exceptional-twisted F2 possibilities have these
2-parts, checked against the cited individual ATLAS order lines:

  G2(2)'=U3(3)           5            https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/clas/U33/
  F4(2)                  24           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/F42/
  E6(2)                  36           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/E62/
  ^2E6(2)                36           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/TE62/
  E7(2)                  63           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/E72/
  E8(2)                  120          https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/E82/
  ^3D4(2)                12           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/TD42/
  ^2F4(2)'               11           https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/exc/TF42/

G2(2) and ^2F4(2) themselves have an additional index-two factor and
are not simple; their derived simple groups are the entries shown.
The Suzuki group ^2B2(2) has order 20 and is not nonabelian simple.
The Ree family ^2G2 has characteristic three and is not an F2 family.
Small classical derived exceptions likewise have orders below the target.
For example, the full groups PSL2(2), PSU3(2), and Sp4(2) have 2-parts
2^1, 2^3, and 2^4 respectively by the displayed formulas; passing to
any derived subgroup or simple quotient cannot increase those exponents.
Thus E8(2) is the only exceptional possibility.

5. EXCLUDING L16(2) AND U16(2) BY TWO ODD PRIMES

The exact primary ATLAS order [A-E7] is

  |E7(2)|=2^63 * 3^11 * 5^2 * 7^3 * 11 * 13 * 17 * 19
                  * 31 * 43 * 73 * 127.

In particular, 19 and 73 divide |C| and therefore must divide |H|.

For L16(2),

  |L16(2)|=2^120 product_(i=2..16)(2^i-1).

The order of 2 modulo 19 is 18. Indeed 2^9=-1 mod 19, while its powers
at 1,2,3,6 are 2,4,8,7, none equal to 1. These test every proper divisor
of 18, and 2^18=1. Thus none of the displayed factors is divisible by 19,
contradicting Lagrange's theorem if H=L16(2).

For U16(2), the determinant factor 3 removes i=1 in the full unitary
formula, and gcd(16,3)=1, so

  |U16(2)|=2^120 product_(i=2..16)(2^i-(-1)^i).

The order of -2 modulo 73 is 18: 2^9=1 mod 73, hence (-2)^9=-1;
its powers at 1,2,3,6 are -2,4,-8,64, none equal to 1. The condition
73 divides 2^i-(-1)^i is exactly (-2)^i=1 mod 73. It never occurs
for 2<=i<=16, again contradicting Lagrange. No assertion about which
unipotent class contains z is needed.

the listed sporadic candidates is 46, strictly below 120.

Although only A9 occurs in the exact theorem, there is also a simple
stronger check excluding every alternating group. Legendre's formula
gives v2(|A_n|)=n-s_2(n)-1, with s_2 the binary digit sum. This value
is nondecreasing in n; it is 119 at n=127 and 126 at n=128, and
therefore never equals 120.

7. EXCLUDING THE L2(2^n +/- 1) FAMILY

In this part of the classification, q=2^n+1 or 2^n-1 must be an odd
prime power. For n>=2, the elementary formula

  |PSL_2(q)|=q(q^2-1)/2

gives v2(|PSL_2(q)|)=n: one of q-1,q+1 is exactly 2^n, and the other
has 2-adic valuation one. The case n=1 is too small and gives no
candidate. Matching (2.1) therefore forces n=120.

Neither resulting number is a prime power:

  2^120-1=(2^60-1)(2^60+1).

The two factors are coprime odd integers greater than one. Similarly,

  2^120+1=(2^40+1)(2^80-2^40+1).

Putting a=2^40, the gcd of a+1 and a^2-a+1 divides 3. But a+1=2 mod 3,
so the gcd is one, and both factors exceed one. This excludes the whole
remaining family without a theorem about dihedral involution centralizers.

8. CONCLUSION AND APPLICATION BOUNDARY

The exact classification list has now been exhausted. Among its candidates,
only E8(2) has compatible order. Hence the group H in the theorem is E8(2).
For reference, the primary ATLAS E8(2) order is

  2^120 * 3^13 * 5^5 * 7^4 * 11^2 * 13^2 * 17^2 * 19 * 31^2
        * 41 * 43 * 73 * 127 * 151 * 241 * 331.

This is a recognition result conditional on the explicit group-theoretic
inputs at the start. It does not turn a subgroup of shape 2^57.E7(2)
into the full centralizer, does not deduce the correct 56-dimensional
module merely from that shape, and does not promote categorical label
permutations to actual automorphisms. In the VOA application those facts,
actual involution fusion, and finiteness must be supplied by the separate
construction and lifting arguments. No character-degree coincidence,
numerical VOA spectrum, or computer search is used in this proof.
