2. TWO ELEMENTARY FINITE-AUTOMORPHISM LEMMAS

Let V be a simple unitary CFT-type VOA with finite Aut(V). Normalize its
positive Hermitian form h by h(1,1)=1, taking h linear in its first
argument. Write theta for the unitary PCT involution. Let B be the unique
normalized invariant symmetric bilinear form, so

  h(x,y)=B(x,theta y),             B(1,1)=1.

The uniqueness and relation are recalled in [L, Remark 2.2]. Every VOA
automorphism preserves B, since its pullback is again normalized and
invariant.

Lemma 2.1. Every element of Aut(V) is unitary for h.

For g in Aut(V), its h-adjoint is

  g^dagger = theta g^(-1) theta.

Indeed h(gx,y)=B(x,g^(-1)theta y)=h(x,theta g^(-1)theta y).
Thus g^dagger is itself a complex-linear VOA automorphism. Consequently
g^dagger g is in the finite group Aut(V). On each finite-dimensional
energy space it is positive definite and self-adjoint, hence diagonalizable
with positive real eigenvalues. Finite order makes those eigenvalues
roots of unity, so every eigenvalue is 1. Thus g^dagger g=1 on V.
Equivalently g commutes with theta. In particular its fixed-point algebra
inherits the unitary structure.

Lemma 2.2. The normalized unitary structure on V is unique, not merely
unique up to an unspecified isomorphism.

Suppose h1,h2 are two normalized unitary forms with PCT involutions
theta1,theta2. Both use the SAME normalized invariant bilinear form B.
The complex-linear map

  p=theta1 theta2

is a VOA automorphism, and

  h2(x,y)=h1(x,p y).

Hermitian symmetry implies that p is h1-self-adjoint. Positivity of h2
implies that p is h1-positive definite on each finite energy space: in
an h1-orthonormal basis the matrix representing h2 has the same positive
eigenvalues as p. Since p lies in finite Aut(V), the preceding eigenvalue
argument gives p=1. Hence theta1=theta2 and h1=h2.

This argument compares compatible PCT involutions using B. It does not
average arbitrary positive forms and assume that VOA invariance survives.

3. UNITARITY OF W32 AND STRONG UNITARITY OF U

The standard positive-definite lattice VOA and its (-1)-twisted module
are unitary by [L, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.8]. Restriction gives unitary
V_BW32^+-modules. The integral twisted summand is a simple current,
and the standard holomorphic W32 extension is therefore preunitarizable.
Gui's simple-current extension theorem [G, Theorem 11.5] gives a unitary
structure on this same W32. Alternatively [L, Theorem 5.1 and the
following paragraph] states this lattice Z2-orbifold conclusion directly;
the underlying theorem has no central-charge-24 restriction.

Aut(W32) is finite by [H, (2.1)]. Lemma 2.1 makes its entire actual
automorphism group unitary. Therefore Delta A and the tensor swap are
unitary on W32 tensor W32, and U inherits a unitary structure.

To establish strong unitarity of U, use actual modules, not character
coincidence alone. The pure-swap-twisted W32 tensor W32 module is unitary
by [G, Proposition 10.13]. Its restriction to U contains representatives
of every nonvacuum Aut(U)-orbit: [H, Theorem 7.2, proof of the character
formulas, and (7.6)] explicitly identifies its simultaneous eigenspaces
as irreducible U-constituents. Their lowest weights include

  2, 3, 4, 7/2, 9/2,

which distinguish the five nonvacuum orbits. Thus every orbit has a
unitarizable representative. Since Aut(U) is finite, Lemma 2.1 makes
all its automorphisms unitary for the inherited form. Transport of a
unitary module along these automorphisms preserves its adjoint identity.
Every irreducible U-module is therefore unitarizable.

Tensor products of the resulting unitary U-modules give unitary
U tensor U-module structures on every summand U_d tensor U_d of X.
No assertion of complete unitarity of U is needed in the proof.

4. THE ACTUAL X AND ITS FIXED ALGEBRA Z ARE UNITARY

Rep(U) is pointed, so the diagonal summands U_d tensor U_d are simple
currents and X is a finite simple-current extension of U tensor U.
The preceding tensor-product forms and their orthogonal direct sum make
X preunitarizable as a module over the one fixed unitary base U tensor U.
That base is of CFT type, rational, and C2-cofinite. [G, Theorem 11.5]
therefore supplies a unitary structure on the ACTUAL X extending the
base structure; no change of VOA multiplication is required.

Aut(X) is finite by input (ii). Thus Lemma 2.1 makes the literal sigma
unitary. Its fixed algebra Z is unitary. Aut(Z) is finite by the same
input, so all automorphisms of Z preserve this unitary structure.

The final step is not an invocation of a general orbifold-positivity
theorem. Such an invocation would require a unitary sigma-twisted module
or strong unitarity of Z, neither of which has been assumed here.

5. THREE AXES GIVE A COMMON UNITARY BASE

Put D0=Y^H. This is strongly rational by the solvable fixed-point theorem,
since H is a finite abelian group acting on the already constructed
strongly rational Y. No positive form on Y is used for this conclusion.

Quantum Galois theory gives

  Y=direct-sum_(chi in H*) M_chi,    M_1=D0,
  M_chi fusion M_psi = M_(chi psi).

Here all four M_chi are nonzero, pairwise inequivalent irreducible
D0-modules. The fusion assertion follows from the symmetric tensor
subcategory Rep(H) in [M, Corollary 4.8 and Example 4.13]. Thus Y is a
finite simple-current extension of D0. For each nonidentity zi in H,

  Y^zi = D0 direct-sum M_(chi_i),

where chi_i is the unique nontrivial character trivial on zi. In
particular Z=Y^z0 is a C2 simple-current extension of D0.

The restriction of z1 to Z is a VOA automorphism. By finite Aut(Z) and
Lemma 2.1 it is unitary. Therefore D0=Z^(z1|Z) inherits a unitary form h0.

We next prove Aut(D0) finite; no general theorem asserting that weight
one zero implies finite automorphism group is required. Rationality and
C2-cofiniteness give finitely many irreducible D0-module classes. Let
S be the stabilizer in Aut(D0) of the two-label support of Z. This is a
finite-index subgroup. By [S, Theorem 2.1 / Corollary 2.2], restriction
from the normalizer of the quantum C2 in Aut(Z) is onto S. Aut(Z) is
finite, so S is finite. A group with a finite, finite-index subgroup is
finite. Hence Aut(D0) is finite.

Transport the unitary structure of Z to Y^z1 and Y^z2 using r and r^2.
Because r normalizes H, it preserves D0. Each transported PCT preserves
D0, and the resulting restriction is a normalized unitary structure on
the SAME D0. Lemma 2.2 shows that both restrictions equal h0, with the
same PCT. Equivalently r|D0 is unitary by Lemma 2.1.

Each nontrivial character chi of H has a kernel of order two. Thus each
M_chi is a submodule of exactly one of the three unitary fixed algebras
Y^zi. Restrict its positive form there to obtain a unitary D0-module
structure. The base structure is h0 in ALL THREE cases. The trivial
module D0 is itself unitary. Taking an orthogonal direct sum therefore
makes Y unitarizable as a D0-module.

6. APPLICATION OF THE PRECISE EXTENSION THEOREM

Every hypothesis of [G, Theorem 11.5] now holds with base D0 and extension Y:

  - D0 is of CFT type, unitary, rational, and C2-cofinite;
  - Y is the existing simple VOA and a finite simple-current extension;
  - Y is unitarizable as a D0-module for the one fixed base form h0.

That theorem extends h0 uniquely to a unitary VOA form on Y. Each h in H
fixes D0 pointwise, so pulling back the final form by h gives another
unitary extension of h0. Uniqueness therefore makes every h in H unitary,
and the final PCT preserves each Y^zi. Its restriction there is consequently
a unitary extension of h0. Theorem 11.5 applied to each two-summand
extension now shows that these restrictions agree with the transported
forms on all three Y^zi.

In particular this supplies unitarity of the actual Y and positivity
of its integral sigma-twisted constituent as a Z-module, without having
postulated that positivity in the argument. Since Y is holomorphic,
complete unitarity also follows immediately from its sole irreducible
module being itself. No conclusion about strong locality follows here.
